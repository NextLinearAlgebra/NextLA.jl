\section{Adaptive Global Row-Basis TLR GEMM}
\label{sec:adaptive-global-row-basis-gemm}

We consider the tile-low-rank (TLR) update
\[
C \leftarrow \alpha AB+\beta C,
\qquad
A_{i\ell}\approx U_{i\ell}^{A}(V_{i\ell}^{A})^{\mathsf T},\quad
B_{\ell j}\approx U_{\ell j}^{B}(V_{\ell j}^{B})^{\mathsf T},\quad
C_{ij}\approx U_{ij}^{C}(V_{ij}^{C})^{\mathsf T},
\]
with tiles $A_{i\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{b_m\times b_k}$,
$B_{\ell j}\in\mathbb{R}^{b_k\times b_n}$,
$C_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{b_m\times b_n}$ on a block grid
$i=1{:}q_m$, $\ell=1{:}q_k$, $j=1{:}q_n$.
All left factors are stored with orthonormal columns,
$(U^{X})^{\mathsf T}U^{X}=I$, so tile norms live in the right factors:
$\|B_{\ell j}\|_F=\|V_{\ell j}^{B}\|_F$.
Tile ranks are $r_{A,i\ell}$, $r_{B,\ell j}$, $r_{C,ij}$, and the structural
widths of a block row of $A$ and a block column of $B$ are
\[
\rho_i:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_k}r_{A,i\ell},
\qquad
\gamma_j:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_k}r_{B,\ell j}.
\]

The algorithm proceeds in phases. Phase~0 computes product-aware weights from
rank metadata and stored norms. Phase~1 builds, per block row of $C$, a
\emph{certified} shared orthonormal basis for the left factors of $A$.
Phase~2 routes every output tile to one of three paths:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{R1}] retain the basis, accumulate the coefficient matrix
  explicitly, compress it exactly;
\item[\textbf{R2}] retain the basis, but the coefficient matrix is itself
  numerically low rank: compress it by a randomized factor-list sketch before
  rotating back;
\item[\textbf{R3}] discard the basis and compress the tile directly from its
  factor list.
\end{itemize}
Phase~3 accumulates in shared coordinates, Phase~4 compresses per tile, and
Phase~5 merges with $\beta C_{ij}$ and writes back in TLR form
(R3 fuses Phases~3--5 into a single factor-list compression).
Algorithm~\ref{alg:driver} is the driver.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{TlrGemm}: adaptive global row-basis TLR GEMM}
\label{alg:driver}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require TLR $A,B,C$; scalars $\alpha,\beta$; tolerances
  $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basis}}$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tile}}$
\State \textbf{Phase 0:} $c_\ell$, $\rho_i$, $\gamma_j$,
  $\mathcal E_i^w$ from rank metadata and $\|V_{\ell j}^{B}\|_F$
\State $\pi\gets$ column order sorting $\gamma_j$ ascending
  \Comment{once per GEMM; see \S\ref{subsec:scheduling}}
\For{each block row $i$ (rows bucketed by $t_i^{\mathrm{screen}}$)}
  \State $(Q_i,\{P_{i\ell}\},t_i)\gets{}$\Call{BuildRowBasis}{$i$}
    \Comment{Alg.~\ref{alg:rowbasis}}
  \State $(J_{\mathrm{R2}},J_{\mathrm{R1}},J_{\mathrm{R3}})\gets{}$\Call{PartitionRow}{$i,\pi$}
    \Comment{Alg.~\ref{alg:route}: three contiguous segments}
  \State accumulate $W_{i\ell j}$ for all $j\in J_{\mathrm{R1}}\cup J_{\mathrm{R2}}$,
    and $M_{ij}$ for $j\in J_{\mathrm{R1}}$
    \Comment{one batch, Alg.~\ref{alg:accumulate}}
  \ForAll{$g\in\{\mathrm{R1},\mathrm{R2}\}$ with $J_g\neq\emptyset$}
    \State $(U^{P},V^{P})_{j\in J_g}\gets{}$\Call{CompressTile}{$g$}
      \Comment{batched over $J_g$, Alg.~\ref{alg:compress}}
  \EndFor
  \State $(U^{C},V^{C})_{j\in J_{\mathrm{R1}}\cup J_{\mathrm{R2}}}\gets{}$\Call{MergeTile}{$\cdot,\beta$}
    \Comment{one batch at padded width, Alg.~\ref{alg:merge}}
  \State compress $\alpha(AB)_{ij}+\beta C_{ij}$ for $j\in J_{\mathrm{R3}}$
    \Comment{batched, Alg.~\ref{alg:fallback}}
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Building Block: Two-Pass Factor-List Sketch}
\label{subsec:factor-list-contraction}

Every randomized branch compresses a matrix available only as a factor list
$X=\sum_{k=1}^{p}L_kR_k^{\mathsf T}$. Both sketch passes contract the long
dimension first,
\[
X\Omega=\sum_{k}L_k(R_k^{\mathsf T}\Omega),
\qquad
X^{\mathsf T}Q=\sum_{k}R_k(L_k^{\mathsf T}Q),
\]
so $X$ is never formed. Working memory is $O\bigl((b_m+b_n)s\bigr)$ rather
than $O(b_mb_n)$, and the list may be regenerated for the second pass instead
of stored. Dense materialization pays only when the sketch width approaches a
tile dimension.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{FactorQB}: two-pass randomized QB of a factor list}
\label{alg:factorqb}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\{(L_k,R_k)\}_{k=1}^{p}$ with
  $X=\sum_kL_kR_k^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$; sketch width $s$
\State draw $\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times s}$;\quad
  $Y\gets\sum_kL_k(R_k^{\mathsf T}\Omega)$
  \Comment{pass 1, never forms $X$}
\State $Q\gets\operatorname{CholQR2}(Y)$
\State $Z\gets\sum_kR_k(L_k^{\mathsf T}Q)$
  \Comment{pass 2: $X\approx QZ^{\mathsf T}$}
\State \Return $(Q,Z)$
  \Comment{caller truncates via a small SVD of $Z$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Phase 0: Product-Aware Row Weights}
\label{subsec:product-aware-weights}

For every inner index $\ell$ define the $B$-row weight
$c_\ell:=\|B_{\ell,:}\|_F=\bigl(\sum_j\|V_{\ell j}^{B}\|_F^2\bigr)^{1/2}$,
computed once per GEMM and shared by all block rows $i$. The weighted row
panel
\[
\overline U_i^{w}
:=
\begin{bmatrix}
c_1U_{i1}^{A} & \cdots & c_{q_k}U_{iq_k}^{A}
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb{R}^{b_m\times\rho_i}
\]
has exactly known energy
$\mathcal E_i^{w}:=\|\overline U_i^{w}\|_F^2=\sum_\ell c_\ell^2\,r_{A,i\ell}$,
because each block has orthonormal columns. The weights make every
$U_{i\ell}^{A}$ contribute to the shared basis in proportion to the block row
of $B$ it will multiply: a direction attached to a negligible $B_{\ell,:}$
does not force rank into the basis.

\subsection{Phase 1: Certified Shared Row Basis}
\label{subsec:certified-row-basis}

The goal is an orthonormal $Q_i\in\mathbb{R}^{b_m\times t_i}$ and coordinates
$P_{i\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{t_i\times r_{A,i\ell}}$ with
$U_{i\ell}^{A}\approx Q_iP_{i\ell}$ --- a column-space factorization
$\overline U_i^{w}\approx Q_iB_i$. The shape of the panel selects the
construction, not the mathematical object. When $\rho_i<b_m$ (tall panel) a
direct $\operatorname{CholQR2}$ at cost $O(b_m\rho_i^2)$ is preferable unless
the expected numerical rank is much smaller than $\rho_i$; when
$\rho_i\ge b_m$ the rank is bounded by $b_m$ and a right-sided randomized QB
is the natural construction.

\paragraph{Blockwise sketch and certificate.}
The weighted panel is never concatenated. With $\Omega_i$ partitioned
conformally ($\Omega_{i\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{A,i\ell}\times s_i}$),
\[
Y_i=\overline U_i^{w}\Omega_i=\sum_\ell c_\ell U_{i\ell}^{A}\Omega_{i\ell},
\qquad
Q_i=\operatorname{CholQR2}(Y_i),
\qquad
P_{i\ell}=Q_i^{\mathsf T}U_{i\ell}^{A},
\]
where the projections are of the \emph{unweighted} factors. The weighted
energy captured so far is $\sum_\ell c_\ell^2\|P_{i\ell}\|_F^2$, so
\[
\delta_i
:=
\mathcal E_i^{w}-\sum_\ell c_\ell^2\|P_{i\ell}\|_F^2
\;\ge 0
\]
is a deterministic a posteriori certificate of the randomized range
approximation, available without touching the panel again.

\paragraph{Adaptive extension.}
While $\delta_i>\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basis}}^2\mathcal E_i^{w}$, the sketch is
extended, not restarted: draw fresh blocks $\Omega_{i\ell}^{\Delta}$, form
$Y_i^{\Delta}=\sum_\ell c_\ell U_{i\ell}^{A}\Omega_{i\ell}^{\Delta}$, and
orthogonalize against the current basis with two-pass block
Gram--Schmidt,
\[
\widehat Y_i^{\Delta}
= Y_i^{\Delta}-Q_i(Q_i^{\mathsf T}Y_i^{\Delta}),
\qquad
\widehat Y_i^{\Delta}
\leftarrow \widehat Y_i^{\Delta}-Q_i(Q_i^{\mathsf T}\widehat Y_i^{\Delta}),
\]
followed by $Q_i^{\Delta}=\operatorname{CholQR2}(\widehat Y_i^{\Delta})$ and
$Q_i\leftarrow[\,Q_i\;\;Q_i^{\Delta}\,]$. Because the basis is extended
rather than rotated, existing coordinates remain valid; only the new rows
$P_{i\ell}^{\Delta}=(Q_i^{\Delta})^{\mathsf T}U_{i\ell}^{A}$ are appended, and
the captured energy — hence $\delta_i$ — updates additively and decreases
monotonically. At worst the sketch spans the panel range, of dimension at most
$\min(b_m,\rho_i)$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{BuildRowBasis}: certified shared basis for block row $i$}
\label{alg:rowbasis}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require weights $c_\ell$, energy $\mathcal E_i^{w}$, tolerance
  $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basis}}$, initial width $s_i$
\If{$\rho_i<b_m$ and no strong rank deficiency expected}
  \State $[\,Q_i,\ \cdot\,]\gets\operatorname{CholQR2}(\overline U_i^{w})$;
    \ $P_{i\ell}\gets Q_i^{\mathsf T}U_{i\ell}^{A}$;
    \ \Return \Comment{direct path, deterministic}
\EndIf
\State $Y_i\gets\sum_\ell c_\ell U_{i\ell}^{A}\Omega_{i\ell}$;\quad
  $Q_i\gets\operatorname{CholQR2}(Y_i)$
\State $P_{i\ell}\gets Q_i^{\mathsf T}U_{i\ell}^{A}$ for all $\ell$;\quad
  $\delta_i\gets\mathcal E_i^{w}-\sum_\ell c_\ell^2\|P_{i\ell}\|_F^2$
\While{$\delta_i>\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basis}}^2\,\mathcal E_i^{w}$}
  \State $Y_i^{\Delta}\gets\sum_\ell c_\ell U_{i\ell}^{A}\Omega_{i\ell}^{\Delta}$
    \Comment{fresh random blocks}
  \State $\widehat Y_i^{\Delta}\gets$ two-pass BCGS of $Y_i^{\Delta}$
    against $Q_i$
  \State $Q_i^{\Delta}\gets\operatorname{CholQR2}(\widehat Y_i^{\Delta})$;\quad
    $Q_i\gets[\,Q_i\;\;Q_i^{\Delta}\,]$
  \State append rows
    $P_{i\ell}^{\Delta}=(Q_i^{\Delta})^{\mathsf T}U_{i\ell}^{A}$;\quad
    $\delta_i\gets\delta_i-\sum_\ell c_\ell^2\|P_{i\ell}^{\Delta}\|_F^2$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $(Q_i,\{P_{i\ell}\},t_i=\text{width}(Q_i))$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Phase 2: Routing}
\label{subsec:row-basis-decision}

A certified basis is not necessarily an economical one; two different
questions must be kept apart.

\paragraph{Row-level screening.}
Applying the panel to a thin $G\in\mathbb{R}^{\rho_i\times s}$ costs
$2b_m\rho_i s$ directly and $2t_i(b_m+\rho_i)s$ through $Q_i(P_iG)$, so the
factorized \emph{representation} is cheaper iff
$t_i<t_i^{\mathrm{screen}}:=b_m\rho_i/(b_m+\rho_i)$.
This compares two ways of applying the same panel; it is \emph{not} the
algorithmic crossover, because the retained path never applies the panel to a
width-$\rho_i$ operand --- accumulation happens in coefficient space, where the
operand width is $\gamma_j$. Since $t_i^{\mathrm{screen}}$ depends only on
row-level data, it is used to pre-sort rows into batches of similar shape,
never to choose the compression path.

\paragraph{Tile-level cost model.}
Fix $(i,j)$; let $s$ be the sketch width of whichever randomized branch is
taken, $R$ the final output rank, and
$r_{M,ij}=\operatorname{rank}(M_{ij})$. Dropping the coupling products
$S_{i\ell j}$ common to all branches, the three paths cost
\begin{align}
\text{(R1) retain, form } M_{ij}:\quad
&2b_nt_i\gamma_j + 4b_nt_i^2 + 2b_mt_iR,
\notag\\
\text{(R2) retain, sketch } M_{ij}:\quad
&4s\,\gamma_j(b_n+t_i) + 2b_mt_ir_{M,ij},
\label{eq:tile-costs}\\
\text{(R3) discard, sketch the tile:}\quad
&4s\,\rho_i(b_m+b_n).
\notag
\end{align}
The structural point sits in the last two lines: R2's sketch passes traverse
$\gamma_j$ columns at height $t_i$, R3's traverse $\rho_i$ columns at height
$b_m$. The retain/discard decision is therefore governed by $\gamma_j$ against
$\rho_i$ as much as by $t_i$ --- and $\gamma_j$ is not available at row level.
Equating R2 and R3 with $r_{M,ij}\approx s$ gives the crossover
\[
t_i^{\mathrm{tile}}
:=
\frac{2\bigl[\rho_i(b_m+b_n)-\gamma_jb_n\bigr]}{2\gamma_j+b_m}
\;\xrightarrow[\;b_m=b_n=b,\ \gamma_j\approx\rho_i=\rho\;]{}\;
\frac{2(\rho+b)}{2\rho+b}\,t_i^{\mathrm{screen}},
\]
with $2(\rho+b)/(2\rho+b)\in[1,2]$: the representation-level comparison is
uniformly conservative, discarding the basis up to a factor of two in $t_i$
earlier than cost justifies. A safety factor $\eta_{\mathrm{row}}$ on
$t_i^{\mathrm{screen}}$ should accordingly be tuned \emph{above} unity.

\paragraph{Coefficient-compression crossover.}
Forming $M_{ij}$ explicitly costs $\simeq 2b_nt_i\gamma_j$ plus an exact
compression at $4b_nt_i^2$; a two-pass factor-list sketch of width $s_{M,ij}$
costs $\simeq 4\gamma_js_{M,ij}(b_n+t_i)$, and both branches pay the rotation
$2b_mt_ir_{M,ij}$. With $t_i\ll b_n$ the sketch wins when
\[
s_{M,ij}
\;\lesssim\;
\eta_M\,\frac{b_nt_i}{b_n+t_i},
\qquad
\eta_M\approx\tfrac12 .
\]
Combined with $s_{M,ij}\approx\hat r_{M,ij}+p$ and $\hat r_{M,ij}\le\gamma_j$,
the condition $\gamma_j\lesssim t_i/2$ is sufficient and decidable from rank
metadata alone.

\paragraph{Decision tree.}
The coefficient-rank test is free, so it is evaluated first, using the
metadata bound
$\hat r_{M,ij}:=\min(t_i,\,b_n,\,\gamma_j)\ \ge\ r_{M,ij}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $\hat r_{M,ij}+p\lesssim\tfrac12t_i$, take \textbf{R2};
\item else if $t_i\le\eta_{\mathrm{row}}\,t_i^{\mathrm{tile}}$, take \textbf{R1};
\item else take \textbf{R3}.
\end{enumerate}
Because $\hat r_{M,ij}$ is a guarantee, condition~1 \emph{certifies} that
coefficient sketching wins; above the threshold the rank is unknown and
forming $M_{ij}$ is the safe default. Two properties keep the ordering cheap.
First, the couplings $S_{i\ell j}$ are shared by all branches --- they enter R1
and R2 through $W_{i\ell j}$ and R3 through $R_{i\ell j}^{A}$ --- so a late
switch to R3 wastes nothing. Second, R1 needs no sketch at all: with $Q_i$
orthonormal, $M_{ij}$ is compressed exactly by a $\operatorname{CholQR2}$ and
a $t_i\times t_i$ SVD, with no sampling error; exactness is a positive reason
to prefer R1 near the boundary. Finally, since $t_i\le b_m$ always, R2's
passes are never taller than R3's, and the only real cost of retaining the
basis is the rotation $2b_mt_ir_{M,ij}$; R3 is warranted mainly when
$\gamma_j\gg\rho_i$ or when $t_i\to b_m$ and $Q_i$ degenerates into a
near-complete rotation. Implementations should not provision R3 as the common
case.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{PartitionRow}: route selection for a whole block row
  (metadata only, no device reads)}
\label{alg:route}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $t_i$, $\rho_i$, $\gamma$-sorted column order $\pi$, oversampling
  $p$, safety $\eta_{\mathrm{row}}>1$, minimum group size $g_{\min}$
\Function{Route}{$j$} \Comment{per-tile predicate, evaluated on the host}
  \State $\hat r\gets\min(t_i,b_n,\gamma_j)$
  \If{$\hat r+p\le t_i/2$} \Return R2
    \Comment{certified: coefficient sketch wins}
  \ElsIf{$t_i\le\eta_{\mathrm{row}}\,
    \dfrac{2[\rho_i(b_m+b_n)-\gamma_jb_n]}{2\gamma_j+b_m}$} \Return R1
    \Comment{basis economical: form $M_{ij}$, exact}
  \Else{} \Return R3 \Comment{basis uneconomical for this tile}
  \EndIf
\EndFunction
\State both predicates are monotone in $\gamma_j$, so locate the two
  boundaries in $\pi$ by binary search
\State $J_{\mathrm{R2}},J_{\mathrm{R1}},J_{\mathrm{R3}}\gets$ the three
  resulting contiguous segments
\State fold any segment with $|J_g|<g_{\min}$ into an adjacent route
  (prefer R1)
\State \Return $(J_{\mathrm{R2}},J_{\mathrm{R1}},J_{\mathrm{R3}})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Scheduling: Routing Without Losing Batching}
\label{subsec:scheduling}

Per-tile routing must not become per-tile \emph{execution}. On a throughput
device the cost of a tile-at-a-time inner loop is dominated by kernel launch
latency and by any host synchronization inside it, not by arithmetic; the
correct pattern is \emph{route, partition, then batch each group}. Two
properties of the decision rule make this possible.

\paragraph{Routing is metadata-only.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:route} reads $t_i,\rho_i,\gamma_j,b_m,b_n,p$ and nothing
else: no factor data, hence no device reads and no synchronization. The routing
map for an entire block row --- indeed for the entire product --- is therefore
computed on the host before any tile work begins.

\paragraph{Routes are monotone in $\gamma_j$.}
Within a block row, $t_i$ and $\rho_i$ are fixed and only $\gamma_j$ varies.
The R2 test requires $\min(t_i,b_n,\gamma_j)+p\lesssim t_i/2$, hence
$\gamma_j\lesssim t_i/2$; and since
\[
\frac{\partial t_i^{\mathrm{tile}}}{\partial\gamma_j}
=
-\,\frac{2b_mb_n+4\rho_i(b_m+b_n)}{(2\gamma_j+b_m)^2}
\;<\;0
\]
strictly, the R1 test $t_i\le\eta_{\mathrm{row}}t_i^{\mathrm{tile}}$ holds for
small $\gamma_j$ and fails monotonically as $\gamma_j$ grows. The three routes
therefore partition each block row into \emph{contiguous segments} ordered by
$\gamma_j$,
\[
\underbrace{\text{R2}}_{\gamma_j\text{ small}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\text{R1}}_{\gamma_j\text{ medium}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\text{R3}}_{\gamma_j\text{ large}},
\]
separated by two boundaries. Because $\gamma_j$ depends only on block column
$j$ of $B$, the ordering is row-independent: it is computed once per GEMM at
cost $O(q_n\log q_n)$ on the host and reused by every row, with only the two
boundaries moving as $t_i$ and $\rho_i$ change. The permutation governs the
order in which columns are \emph{processed}, not how they are stored, so
panel accessors remain zero-copy.

\paragraph{Consequences for the executor.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Group-wise execution.} A row issues at most three batched groups
instead of $q_n$ singletons; when a row is uniform in $\gamma_j$ it issues
one, recovering the fully batched schedule exactly. The couplings
$S_{i\ell j}$ and the coefficients $W_{i\ell j}$ are shared by all routes and
are accumulated in a single batch spanning the whole row.
\item \emph{Minimum group size.} A segment shorter than a device-dependent
threshold is folded into an adjacent route rather than launched on its own;
all three routes are correct for every tile, so this trades a known small
arithmetic overhead for a launch. R1 is the natural absorber, being exact and
requiring no sketch.
\item \emph{Padding within a group.} Sketch widths $s_{M,ij}$ and output
widths vary with $\gamma_j$ inside a group. Since the segments are already
$\gamma$-contiguous the spread is small, so groups are padded to their
maximum width rather than sub-bucketed; padded columns are exactly zero and
do not perturb the result.
\item \emph{Merge across routes.} R1 and R2 tiles reach Phase~5 with different
$U_{ij}^{P}$ widths. Padding those to a common width leaves the merge algebra
unchanged, so the whole row merges in one batch. R3 tiles bypass Phase~5,
having already absorbed $\beta C_{ij}$.
\item \emph{Cross-row batching.} Rows are additionally bucketed by
$t_i^{\mathrm{screen}}$, which is the purpose of that row-level quantity:
rows of similar shape execute their groups together, so basis application and
merge batches span several rows.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Phase 3: Accumulation in Shared Coordinates}
\label{subsec:shared-coordinate-accumulation}

With $U_{i\ell}^{A}\approx Q_iP_{i\ell}$,
\[
A_{i\ell}B_{\ell j}
\approx
Q_i\,P_{i\ell}(V_{i\ell}^{A})^{\mathsf T}U_{\ell j}^{B}(V_{\ell j}^{B})^{\mathsf T},
\]
so with
$W_{i\ell j}
:= (U_{\ell j}^{B})^{\mathsf T}V_{i\ell}^{A}P_{i\ell}^{\mathsf T}
\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{B,\ell j}\times t_i}$
the product contribution is
\[
\alpha(AB)_{ij}\approx Q_iM_{ij}^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
M_{ij}
:=\alpha\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_k}V_{\ell j}^{B}W_{i\ell j}
\in\mathbb{R}^{b_n\times t_i}.
\]
The product defining $W_{i\ell j}$ is parenthesized by the smaller of $t_i$
and $r_{A,i\ell}$: if $t_i\le r_{A,i\ell}$, precompute
$T_{i\ell}:=V_{i\ell}^{A}P_{i\ell}^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb{R}^{b_k\times t_i}$
once per $(i,\ell)$ and evaluate
$W_{i\ell j}=(U_{\ell j}^{B})^{\mathsf T}T_{i\ell}$; otherwise form
$S_{i\ell j}:=(V_{i\ell}^{A})^{\mathsf T}U_{\ell j}^{B}$ and
$W_{i\ell j}=S_{i\ell j}^{\mathsf T}P_{i\ell}^{\mathsf T}$.
No recompression is performed inside the $\ell$-sum; all contributions
accumulate in the common coordinates of $Q_i$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{Accumulate}: coefficients for row $i$, column $j$}
\label{alg:accumulate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\If{$t_i\le r_{A,i\ell}$ for the row's typical rank}
  \State $T_{i\ell}\gets V_{i\ell}^{A}P_{i\ell}^{\mathsf T}$ once per
    $(i,\ell)$;\quad
    $W_{i\ell j}\gets(U_{\ell j}^{B})^{\mathsf T}T_{i\ell}$
\Else
  \State $S_{i\ell j}\gets(V_{i\ell}^{A})^{\mathsf T}U_{\ell j}^{B}$;\quad
    $W_{i\ell j}\gets S_{i\ell j}^{\mathsf T}P_{i\ell}^{\mathsf T}$
\EndIf
\If{route $=$ R1}
  \State $M_{ij}\gets\alpha\sum_\ell V_{\ell j}^{B}W_{i\ell j}$
    \Comment{explicit $b_n\times t_i$ accumulation}
\Else
  \State keep the factor list
    $M_{ij}=\sum_\ell(\alpha V_{\ell j}^{B})(W_{i\ell j}^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}$
    \Comment{R2: never form $M_{ij}$}
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Phase 4: Per-Tile Compression}
\label{subsec:coefficient-compression}

Since $Q_i$ has orthonormal columns,
$\operatorname{rank}(Q_iM_{ij}^{\mathsf T})=\operatorname{rank}(M_{ij})$: a
wide basis does not force a high-rank tile. $M_{ij}$ is numerically low rank
whenever the $B$-side activates only a small subspace of $Q_i$ or the
contributions across $\ell$ are strongly correlated --- this is what R2
exploits.

\paragraph{R1 (exact).}
Compress the explicit $M_{ij}$ by $\operatorname{CholQR2}$ plus a
$t_i\times t_i$ SVD:
$M_{ij}\approx V_{ij}^{M}\Sigma_{ij}^{M}(P_{ij}^{M})^{\mathsf T}$ with
$P_{ij}^{M}$ orthonormal.

\paragraph{R2 (factor-list sketch).}
Apply Algorithm~\ref{alg:factorqb} to the list
$\{(\alpha V_{\ell j}^{B},\,W_{i\ell j}^{\mathsf T})\}_\ell$ with width
$s_{M,ij}\approx\hat r_{M,ij}+p$; a small SVD of $Z^{\mathsf T}$ then yields
the same three factors.

\medskip
In both cases the tile is reduced from width $t_i$ to $r_{M,ij}$ entirely in
coefficient space, and the basis is applied once at the end:
\[
Q_iM_{ij}^{\mathsf T}
\approx
\underbrace{(Q_iP_{ij}^{M})}_{U_{ij}^{P}}
\underbrace{\bigl(V_{ij}^{M}\Sigma_{ij}^{M}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}}_{(V_{ij}^{P})^{\mathsf T}},
\qquad
(Q_iP_{ij}^{M})^{\mathsf T}(Q_iP_{ij}^{M})=I,
\]
the orthonormality following from that of $Q_i$ and $P_{ij}^{M}$. On the
plain R1 path with no truncation one simply takes $U_{ij}^{P}=Q_i$,
$V_{ij}^{P}=M_{ij}$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{CompressTile}: product tile in factored form}
\label{alg:compress}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\If{route $=$ R1}
  \State $(V^{M},\Sigma^{M},P^{M})\gets$ CholQR2 $+$ small SVD of $M_{ij}$
    \Comment{exact, no sampling}
\ElsIf{route $=$ R2}
  \State $(Q^{M},Z^{M})\gets{}$\Call{FactorQB}{$\{(\alpha V_{\ell j}^{B},W_{i\ell j}^{\mathsf T})\}_\ell$, $s_{M,ij}$}
  \State $(V^{M},\Sigma^{M},P^{M})\gets$ small SVD of $(Z^{M})^{\mathsf T}$
\EndIf
\State \Return $U_{ij}^{P}=Q_iP^{M}$,\quad $V_{ij}^{P}=V^{M}\Sigma^{M}$
  \Comment{one rotation through $Q_i$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Phase 5: Merge with the Existing Output Tile}
\label{subsec:merge-output-tile}

Let the product contribution be
$\alpha(AB)_{ij}\approx U_{ij}^{P}(V_{ij}^{P})^{\mathsf T}$ with
$U_{ij}^{P}$ orthonormal. The updated tile
$X_{ij}:=\alpha(AB)_{ij}+\beta C_{ij}$ is merged by splitting the old basis
across the new one:
\[
D_{ij}:=(U_{ij}^{P})^{\mathsf T}U_{ij}^{C},
\qquad
U_{ij}^{\perp}:=U_{ij}^{C}-U_{ij}^{P}D_{ij}
\approx Q_{ij}^{\perp}R_{ij}^{\perp},
\]
the last factorization via a stable two-pass orthogonalization with
$(U_{ij}^{P})^{\mathsf T}Q_{ij}^{\perp}\approx0$. Then
\[
X_{ij}\approx\widetilde Q_{ij}\widetilde M_{ij}^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\widetilde Q_{ij}
:=\bigl[\,U_{ij}^{P}\;\;Q_{ij}^{\perp}\,\bigr],
\qquad
\widetilde M_{ij}
:=\bigl[\,V_{ij}^{P}+\beta V_{ij}^{C}D_{ij}^{\mathsf T}
   \;\;\;\beta V_{ij}^{C}(R_{ij}^{\perp})^{\mathsf T}\,\bigr].
\]
Because $\widetilde Q_{ij}$ is orthonormal, compressing the tile reduces to
compressing coordinates:
$\|\widetilde Q_{ij}\widetilde M_{ij}^{\mathsf T}
   -\widetilde Q_{ij}PN^{\mathsf T}\|_F
 =\|\widetilde M_{ij}^{\mathsf T}-PN^{\mathsf T}\|_F$.
With $\widetilde M_{ij}^{\mathsf T}\approx P_{ij}N_{ij}^{\mathsf T}$
($P_{ij}$ orthonormal), the stored factors are
$U_{ij}^{C}=\widetilde Q_{ij}P_{ij}$ and $V_{ij}^{C}=N_{ij}$. When
$\widetilde Q_{ij}$ is narrow this compression is done deterministically
(CholQR2 $+$ small SVD); when moderate, the tile may instead be compressed
from the two-term factor list
$\{(U_{ij}^{P},V_{ij}^{P}),(U_{ij}^{C},\beta V_{ij}^{C})\}$ without forming
$\widetilde Q_{ij}$ or $\widetilde M_{ij}$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{MergeTile}: fold $\beta C_{ij}$ into the product}
\label{alg:merge}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\If{$\beta=0$ or $r_{C,ij}=0$}
  \State \Return $(U_{ij}^{P},V_{ij}^{P})$ after rank truncation
\EndIf
\State $D\gets(U_{ij}^{P})^{\mathsf T}U_{ij}^{C}$;\quad
  $U^{\perp}\gets U_{ij}^{C}-U_{ij}^{P}D$
\State $(Q^{\perp},R^{\perp})\gets$ two-pass orthogonalization of $U^{\perp}$
  against $U_{ij}^{P}$
\State $\widetilde Q\gets[\,U_{ij}^{P}\;\;Q^{\perp}\,]$;\quad
  $\widetilde M\gets[\,V_{ij}^{P}+\beta V_{ij}^{C}D^{\mathsf T}
    \;\;\beta V_{ij}^{C}(R^{\perp})^{\mathsf T}\,]$
\State $(P,N)\gets$ compress $\widetilde M^{\mathsf T}$
  \Comment{CholQR2 $+$ small SVD, or two-term \textsc{FactorQB}}
\State \Return $U_{ij}^{C}=\widetilde QP$,\quad $V_{ij}^{C}=N$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{The Discard Route (R3): Direct Factor-List Compression}
\label{subsec:direct-factor-list-fallback}

When the shared basis is uneconomical for a tile, the tile is compressed
directly from its original factors. With
$S_{i\ell j}:=(V_{i\ell}^{A})^{\mathsf T}U_{\ell j}^{B}$ and
$R_{i\ell j}^{A}:=\alpha V_{\ell j}^{B}S_{i\ell j}^{\mathsf T}$, each product
term is $\alpha A_{i\ell}B_{\ell j}\approx
U_{i\ell}^{A}(R_{i\ell j}^{A})^{\mathsf T}$, so the complete updated tile has
the factor list
\[
X_{ij}
\approx
\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_k}U_{i\ell}^{A}(R_{i\ell j}^{A})^{\mathsf T}
+
U_{ij}^{C}(\beta V_{ij}^{C})^{\mathsf T},
\]
to which Algorithm~\ref{alg:factorqb} applies verbatim with
$L=\{U_{i\ell}^{A}\}\cup\{U_{ij}^{C}\}$ and
$R=\{R_{i\ell j}^{A}\}\cup\{\beta V_{ij}^{C}\}$, followed by a small SVD.
The compressor acts on the \emph{complete} updated tile
$\alpha(AB)_{ij}+\beta C_{ij}$, never on the product alone followed by a
second merge compression; for $\beta=0$ the distinction disappears.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{DirectTile} (R3): compress the updated tile from factors}
\label{alg:fallback}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $R_{i\ell j}^{A}\gets\alpha V_{\ell j}^{B}S_{i\ell j}^{\mathsf T}$
  for all $\ell$ \Comment{$S_{i\ell j}$ shared with the retained routes}
\State $(Q^{\mathrm{out}},Z)\gets{}$\Call{FactorQB}{$\{(U_{i\ell}^{A},R_{i\ell j}^{A})\}_\ell\cup\{(U_{ij}^{C},\beta V_{ij}^{C})\}$, $s_{ij}$}
\State small SVD of $Z$; truncate; store
  $U_{ij}^{C}\gets Q^{\mathrm{out}}(\cdot)$, $V_{ij}^{C}\gets(\cdot)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{The Column-Side Dual and the Two-Sided Construction}
\label{sec:dual}

Everything above shares a basis across the tiles of a block \emph{row} of
$C$, built from the left factors of $A$. The mirror construction shares a
basis across a block \emph{column} of $C$, built from the right factors of
$B$. The two are mathematically symmetric; the useful questions are which
asymmetries are real and whether the constructions compose.

\subsection{The Dual Construction}

For fixed $j$, define the weighted column panel
\[
\overline V_j^{\,d}
:=
\begin{bmatrix}
d_1V_{1j}^{B} & \cdots & d_{q_k}V_{q_kj}^{B}
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb{R}^{b_n\times\gamma_j},
\qquad
d_\ell:=\|A_{:,\ell}\|_F
=\Bigl(\sum_i\|V_{i\ell}^{A}\|_F^2\Bigr)^{1/2},
\]
the norm identity following from orthonormality of $U_{i\ell}^{A}$ exactly as
$c_\ell$ followed from that of $U_{\ell j}^{B}$. One seeks orthonormal
$G_j\in\mathbb{R}^{b_n\times u_j}$ and coordinates $N_{\ell j}$ with
$V_{\ell j}^{B}\approx G_jN_{\ell j}$; the sketch, the BCGS2 extension, and
the certificate are identical in form to Phase~1. The product contribution
becomes
\[
\alpha(AB)_{ij}
\approx
\widetilde M_{ij}G_j^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\widetilde M_{ij}
:=\alpha\sum_{\ell}U_{i\ell}^{A}S_{i\ell j}N_{\ell j}^{\mathsf T}
\in\mathbb{R}^{b_m\times u_j},
\]
and since $G_j$ is orthonormal, tile compression again reduces to compressing
the coordinate matrix.

\subsection{A Priori Side Selection}
\label{subsec:side-selection}

The two constructions solve the same problem in different spaces, and for a
one-sided algorithm exactly one of them must be chosen --- ideally from tile
shapes and rank metadata alone, before any factor is touched. Write the
structural widths and the \emph{a priori basis capacities} as
\[
\rho=q_kr_A,\qquad \gamma=q_kr_B,
\qquad
\bar t:=\min(b_m,\rho),
\qquad
\bar u:=\min(b_n,\gamma),
\]
$\bar t$ and $\bar u$ being the widths reached when the panel carries no
exploitable structure (the pessimistic case; $q_k=k/b_k$). Amortizing each
basis over the tiles it serves, the per-tile cost of the two sides is
\begin{align}
\text{primal (row basis, $b_m$-space):}\quad
&\underbrace{2b_n\gamma\bar t}_{\text{accumulate}}
+\underbrace{4b_m\rho\bar t/q_n}_{\text{build}}
+\underbrace{2b_m\bar tR}_{\text{expand}},
\notag\\
\text{dual (column basis, $b_n$-space):}\quad
&\underbrace{2b_m\rho\bar u}_{\text{accumulate}}
+\underbrace{4b_n\gamma\bar u/q_m}_{\text{build}}
+\underbrace{2b_n\bar uR}_{\text{expand}}.
\notag
\end{align}
The build terms carry a factor $1/q_n$ (resp.\ $1/q_m$) and are negligible
once the grid exceeds a few blocks in each direction, so the choice is
governed by the accumulation terms. Their comparison gives the selection rule
\[
\boxed{\;
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{side}}
:=
\frac{b_n\gamma\bar t}{b_m\rho\bar u}
=
\underbrace{\frac{b_n}{b_m}}_{\text{tile aspect}}
\cdot
\underbrace{\frac{r_B}{r_A}}_{\text{rank ratio}}
\cdot
\underbrace{\frac{\bar t}{\bar u}}_{\text{capacity ratio}}
\;<\;1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\text{use the row (primal) basis.}
\;}
\]
The essential asymmetry is that \emph{each side accumulates against the other
operand}: the primal contracts over $\gamma$ at height $b_n$, the dual over
$\rho$ at height $b_m$. Two limits make the rule concrete.

\paragraph{Unsaturated panels ($q_kr_A<b_m$ and $q_kr_B<b_n$).}
Then $\bar t=\rho$, $\bar u=\gamma$, the capacity ratio is $r_A/r_B$, and
$\mathcal R_{\mathrm{side}}=b_n/b_m$: choose the basis in the \emph{longer}
tile dimension, so that accumulation runs over the shorter one. For a tall
output ($b_m>b_n$) the row basis wins; for a wide output the column basis
does. Ranks cancel entirely.

\paragraph{Saturated panels ($q_kr_A\geq b_m$ and $q_kr_B\geq b_n$).}
Then $\bar t=b_m$, $\bar u=b_n$, the capacity ratio is $b_m/b_n$, and
$\mathcal R_{\mathrm{side}}=r_B/r_A$: tile shape cancels and the choice is
made by rank alone --- build the basis on the side facing the \emph{lower-rank}
operand, since that operand sets the accumulation width. In the mixed regimes
the boxed formula interpolates between the two.

\paragraph{Scope and tie-breakers.}
Because $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{side}}$ is built from capacities, it is a
worst-case ranking: if one side possesses genuine shared structure its
achieved $t_i\ll\bar t$ and it wins irrespective of the estimate. The rule
should therefore select which side to \emph{attempt first}, with the Phase-1
certificate $\delta$ confirming the choice or triggering a switch on a sample
of rows and columns. Two considerations sit outside the cost model and
override it when they apply: the conditioning of the dual panel (below), and
which side is coherent at all (Section~\ref{subsec:dual-coherence}). For
small grids, where the build terms survive, the comparison additionally
favours the side with fewer panels --- $q_m$ for the primal against $q_n$ for
the dual.

\subsection{Genuine Asymmetries}

\paragraph{Certificate cost.}
The row-side energy $\|\overline U_i\|_F^2=\rho_i$ is pure rank metadata; the
column-side energy needs the block norms of $B$, which Phase~0 computes
anyway. The practical difference is that the row-side quantity exists before
any data is touched, which is what allows $\rho_i$ to drive scheduling and
batching.

\paragraph{Conditioning.}
The row panel concatenates blocks with orthonormal columns, so its
conditioning is set by subspace geometry alone. The column panel inherits the
dynamic range of the block norms $\|B_{\ell j}\|_F$ --- orders of magnitude in
a hierarchical matrix --- and $\operatorname{CholQR2}$ squares the condition
number; the dual should use a shifted variant or block-normalized sketching.

\paragraph{Coherence.}
\label{subsec:dual-coherence}
The deciding factor is usually neither. A shared row basis exists when the
block row of $A$ has a common range; a shared column basis when the block
column of $B$ does. For a symmetric kernel matrix squared, both hold; for
$A$ from an $\mathcal H^2$ discretization times a general TLR correction
$B$, only one side succeeds, and the matrices decide. Since Phase~1 returns
$t_i$ (resp.\ $u_j$) with a certificate, both sides can be probed on a sample
of rows and columns and the distributions of $t_i/\rho_i$ and $u_j/\gamma_j$
compared directly.

\subsection{The Two-Sided Construction}

If both bases are available they compose:
\[
\alpha(AB)_{ij}
\approx
Q_i\Gamma_{ij}G_j^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\Gamma_{ij}
:=\alpha\sum_{\ell}P_{i\ell}S_{i\ell j}N_{\ell j}^{\mathsf T}
\in\mathbb{R}^{t_i\times u_j}.
\]
The accumulation now costs $2q_kt_ir_A(r_B+u_j)$ per tile and contains no
tile dimension at all; against the primal $2b_n\gamma_jt_i$ the saving is of
order $b_n/u_j$. Recovering flat TLR storage takes an SVD of the
$t_i\times u_j$ matrix $\Gamma_{ij}$ and two rotations
$U_{ij}^{C}=Q_i\Theta_{ij}$, $V_{ij}^{C}=G_j\Xi_{ij}\Sigma_{ij}$ at cost
$2R(b_mt_i+b_nu_j)$ --- paid once per tile, not once per inner index, so the
saving survives whenever $\gamma_jb_n\gg Rb_m$, the normal regime.

The form $Q_i\Gamma_{ij}G_j^{\mathsf T}$, with $Q_i$ shared down a block row
and $G_j$ along a block column, is precisely an $\mathcal H^2$/BLR$^2$
representation: the algorithm reconstructs at run time, with a certificate,
the structure the flat format discards. If the output may retain that form,
the final expansion is skipped and per-tile storage falls from $(b_m+b_n)R$
to $t_iu_j$. The price is that both Phase-1 constructions must succeed for
the tile; the routing of Section~\ref{subsec:row-basis-decision} applies to
each side independently, with the two-sided path taken on the intersection.
